# PROJECT BRIEF: "Oma Approved?"

## 1. CONCEPT
A small, funny website where you chat with a disappointed German grandma. Built for a university Generative AI course. The only deliverable is a live website link, deployed via GitHub and Vercel.

Visitors sit in Oma Hildegard's cozy living room and tell her about their life. She tries to understand the modern world and mostly doesn't. Her approval is rare and precious, tracked by an approval meter. A temperature dial (an old oven knob) controls the model's temperature, which is the core concept from the lecture: low temperature makes her predictable, high temperature makes her chaotic.

The chat is the main part of the page. Everything around it creates the cozy grandma's house atmosphere.

## 2. THE CHARACTER: OMA HILDEGARD
- Name: Hildegard, 81, from Germany
- Loves: Bavarian TV (Volksmusik shows, Heimatfilme, crime series on Sunday evening) and Schwarzwälder Kirschtorte
- Language: speaks English, but drops German phrases now and then, always followed by the translation in brackets. Example: "Ach du lieber Himmel! (Oh dear heavens!)"
- Tone: funny and skeptical of modern life, but with warm moments. Underneath the disappointment she clearly loves the person.
- Running jokes (use naturally, not all at once):
  - "Früher war alles besser (Everything was better in the old days)"
  - "Back in my day..."
  - "You should eat more."
  - "My phone isn't working."
  - "I deleted the internet."
- Approval: she rarely approves. Modern jobs, apps, trends and lifestyle choices confuse or worry her. Traditional, caring or sensible things (calling family, eating properly, saving money, going to bed early) earn approval.
- Boundary: if someone shares something genuinely serious (grief, illness, crisis, real distress), she drops the act completely and responds with sincere kindness. No jokes, no disapproval, approval meter unaffected. If someone seems to be in real danger, she gently encourages them to talk to someone they trust or a professional.
- Answer length: short, 2 to 4 sentences. Max 200 output tokens.
- Visible disclaimer on the site (footer or small "About" note): "Hildegard is totally not based on my grandma."

## 3. TEMPERATURE DIAL (lecture requirement, must be honest)
- Visual: an old oven knob with three notches labeled 1, 2, 3.
- Mapping to the real API temperature:
  - 1 = Sunday Church Oma (temperature 0.2): strict, predictable, similar answers every time
  - 2 = Everyday Oma (temperature 0.8): her normal self
  - 3 = Eierlikör Oma (temperature 1.4): rambling, surprising, a bit chaotic
- Honest implementation: the dial ONLY changes the temperature parameter sent to the API. The system prompt stays identical for all settings. The difference in behavior must come purely from temperature.
- Show the current mood label next to the dial.
- Small info button ("What does the oven do?") with a short plain-language explanation: low temperature means the model picks the most likely words, high temperature means it takes more creative and random choices.
- Default setting: 2.

## 4. FEATURES (keep it simple)
In scope:
1. Chat as the main element of the page.
2. Approval meter next to Oma. Starts at 30 out of 100. Each Oma reply moves it up or down.
3. Oma's portrait with changing facial expressions next to the meter, based on approval level (see Design).
4. Editable confession cards as conversation starters. Preset cards: "I quit my job to become an influencer", "I'm vegan now", "I moved in with my boyfriend", "I only eat avocado toast", "I work from home in my pyjamas". Clicking a card puts its text into the input field, where the user can edit it before sending.
5. Ending with the recipe: the chat ends either after 15 user messages ("Oma needs her nap now") or when the user clicks a "Say goodbye" button. Before ending, Oma always gives her Schwarzwälder Kirschtorte recipe. The recipe is hardcoded (always identical, because you stick with tradition) and shown as a recipe card. It is NOT generated by the AI. After that, the input is disabled and a "Visit Oma again" button restarts the chat.
6. Message counter shown subtly, e.g. "Oma's patience: 12 cups of tea left".

Out of scope: verdict stamps, challenge modes, report cards, user accounts, databases.

## 5. DESIGN
- Layout: chat is the dominant element. Oma's portrait, approval meter and oven dial sit beside it on desktop, and above it in a compact bar on mobile.
- Vibe: cozy grandma's living room. Floral wallpaper pattern, warm lamp light, doilies, wooden furniture, a cuckoo clock or framed photos as decoration. Warm palette (cream, faded rose, warm brown, muted green).
- Art style: cozy game style (warm, soft, slightly pixel or illustrated look like a cozy farming game). Original character, drawn as inline SVG so no external images are needed.
- Oma's look: typical grandma clothes: cardigan, floral blouse or apron, glasses, grey hair in a bun, maybe a brooch.
- Facial expressions (switch based on approval, smooth transition):
  - 0 to 20: appalled
  - 21 to 40: disappointed
  - 41 to 60: skeptical
  - 61 to 80: pleased
  - 81 to 100: proud and beaming
  - Plus a "caring" expression when she switches into serious, kind mode
- Chat bubbles: Oma's messages look like handwritten notes or recipe cards, user messages simpler.
- Fonts: a handwriting style font for Oma, a readable serif or rounded font for the rest (Google Fonts are fine).
- Mobile friendly is a must. Classmates will open it on their phones.
- Opening message from Oma (fixed, not generated): "Ah, there you are! Sit down, sit down. Have you eaten? Tell me, what are you doing with your life these days?"

## 6. TECHNICAL SETUP
- Stack: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript for the frontend, plus one Vercel serverless function at /api/chat.js. Use fetch to call OpenAI.
- Model: gpt-4.1-mini via the OpenAI Chat Completions API. (GPT-5 mini does not support changing temperature, so it cannot be used.) Make the model name configurable via an optional environment variable OPENAI_MODEL, with gpt-4.1-mini as default.
- API key: read from process.env.OPENAI_API_KEY (already set in Vercel). Never in frontend code, never in any file.
- Approval scoring: the model responds in JSON, e.g. { "reply": "...", "approval_change": integer from -10 to +10, "serious_mode": true/false }, using JSON response format. If parsing fails, show the raw text as the reply and change approval by 0.
- If the API call fails, Oma shows a friendly in-character error like "My phone isn't working again... try once more."

## 7. BUDGET PROTECTION (the API key belongs to my professor, spending must stay minimal)
- max_tokens: 200 per answer
- Only send the system prompt plus the last 8 messages as history
- Max 15 user messages per visit (enforced in the frontend, and the server rejects requests with more than 15 user messages in the history)
- Limit user input to 300 characters
- Server only accepts the three allowed temperatures (0.2, 0.8, 1.4) and rejects anything else
- Basic rate limiting per IP in the serverless function (best effort)

## 8. THE RECIPE (hardcoded in recipe.js, shown at the end)
Oma Hildegard's Schwarzwälder Kirschtorte
"Exactly like my mother made it. Don't you dare change anything."

For the sponge: 6 eggs, 200 g sugar, 1 packet vanilla sugar, 100 g flour, 50 g cornstarch, 50 g cocoa powder, 1 tsp baking powder

For the filling: 1 jar sour cherries (about 700 g, drained, keep the juice), 2 tbsp cornstarch, 2 tbsp sugar, 4 to 6 tbsp Kirschwasser (cherry brandy), 800 ml whipping cream, 2 packets cream stabilizer, 2 packets vanilla sugar

For decoration: chocolate shavings, 12 cocktail cherries

1. Beat eggs, sugar and vanilla sugar for 10 minutes until thick and pale. Sift in flour, cornstarch, cocoa and baking powder and fold in gently.
2. Bake in a 26 cm springform tin at 175 °C for about 30 to 35 minutes. Let it cool completely, ideally overnight, then cut into three layers.
3. Bring 250 ml cherry juice to a boil, thicken with the cornstarch mixed with a little cold juice and the sugar, stir in the cherries and let it cool.
4. Drizzle the layers with Kirschwasser. "Not too little. Not too much. You'll know."
5. Whip the cream with stabilizer and vanilla sugar until stiff.
6. First layer: cherries and some cream. Second layer: cream. Top with the third layer and cover the whole cake with cream.
7. Decorate with 12 cream rosettes, a cherry on each, and chocolate shavings. Chill for at least 3 hours.
